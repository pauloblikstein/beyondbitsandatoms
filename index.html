Beyond Bits and Atoms: Designing Constructionist Learning Technologies

MAKING WITH AI EDITION

In the Beyond Bits & Atoms class, students learn to design, build, and critically examine constructionist educational technologies. During the Spring semester, BBA combines a theory-oriented course, MSTU 5180, with a practice-focused design lab, MSTU 5181.  Over the years, dozens of BBA projects have been published, exhibited, further developed, or transformed into commercial products.

In this 2-week Summer Intensive session, BBA continues to integrate theory and practice, with a stronger emphasis on product design, digital prototyping, and the development of theory-inspired learning tools. The final project is a working prototype of a constructionist educational technology.

Why a "Making with AI" Edition for BBA? Given the exponential growth of AI tools for coding, rapid prototyping, and 3D design, we will pay special attention to their use in class. They will not replace the "real" learning of the various tools in the class, but augment and enhance what students can do, build, and create.

A two-week intensive studio in Designing Constructionist Technologies for Learning

The BBA Summer Studio is an intensive hands-on version of Beyond Bits & Atoms, focused on Making with AI tools. Across ten days, you'll move through the lab's full toolkit — laser cutter, 3D printers, microcontrollers, woodworking, coding, prototyping — and use them to design and build a working educational technology of your own. We read just enough theory to gain a foundational understanding of why we build the way we do, then spend the rest of the day making, building, and giving one another feedback. In the Summer session, students explore the basics of constructivism, constructionism, and critical pedagogy, as well as the role of media and tools, design thinking, and user-centered design. These ideas are studied in the context of case studies of existing educational technologies, and the students' own projects. Apply by June 22nd here.

Summary Program

DAILY ROUTINE

Every day runs from 9:30 to 4 as a studio. Mornings start with a short discussion — based on a short reading or a case study — followed a lab block. After lunch, we will have a second lab block and hands-on project time. Students will be able to use an optional open lab time until 5 pm, and possibly later in the second week of the class.

The first week is a tour of tools and techniques: by Friday you'll have cut, printed, wired, and programmed something, and pitched ideas for a final project. The second week is mostly for project development: you scope a project, build it, do user testing, iterate, and exhibit it at a closing mini-Expo.

What you'll make

Each participant will build one working prototype of a constructionist learning technology — something a learner could actually pick up and use — plus a short write-up documenting the idea, the build, and what testing revealed.

Reading & theory

Reading is deliberately light. A handful of short, foundational pieces frame the work — enough to give the students a point of view and foundational knowledge on the main learning and design theories.

PREVIOUS EXPERIENCE? NO!!

No prior coding, fabrication, or electronics experience is assumed. The studio suits educators, designers, and graduate students from all backgrounds.
